01 · The problem

The question that stops a founder before she starts

the ask: “can I sell this?”

Which foods a home kitchen may sell, at which venues, under which license, and the exact sentence that must appear on the label — every state answers differently, and the answers change. A baker weighing her first farmers-market booth guesses, gives up, or pays for a food-law consult that costs more than her first month of profit.

Then one clause in a labeling section moves, and nobody tells her the label she is printing tonight is now wrong.

A consultant answers once. OvenClear answers with the statute quoted and a hash pinning the exact text it relied on — then does what no consultant does: it keeps the answer true, re-issuing the label when the rule beneath it changes.

“We are not going to sell you a label for this. … Charging you $19 for a label you cannot legally use would be the product failing.”

— the storefront’s actual refusal page, server/pages.ts. Not an error state. It is written like the product’s proudest moment, because it is.

Verdict before payment

The verdict is computed and shown before checkout exists. A prohibited product never reaches a pay button.

Recomputed at checkout

The server re-runs the verdict on its own state at checkout. A hidden-field edit cannot talk it into selling a label the state disallows.

Refund over revenue

If a prohibited order is paid anyway, fulfillment refuses the label and flags a refund rather than keeping the money. The QA gate fails closed after payment too.

03 · The machinery

Question → verdict → label → re-issue, on a signed ledger

law snapshot ac613546c5e5…

The compliance decision is a deterministic table over hash-pinned law snapshots — not a language model. Gemini sits around that core, constrained. Every step appends to a hash-chained ledger with per-agent Ed25519 signatures and daily Merkle roots.

STAGE 1

Intake

Six structured questions, no chat box. “My tangy no-knead boule” hits a deterministic catalog first; Gemini only gets a turn on a miss.

gemini-2.5-flash · enum-locked
STAGE 2

Verdict

The table resolves product, venue, and state against pinned snapshots. Every citation quote is a byte-substring of its snapshot. Unknown category? It refuses rather than guesses.

I1 · pinned or no verdict
STAGE 3

Label + QA gate

The label is composed, never generated. A gate re-reads the finished artifact: every mandated sentence present byte-for-byte, or the label does not ship — paid or not.

I2 · fails closed
STAGE 4

Law-Watch

Snapshots are re-diffed and each delta classified material, cosmetic, or immaterial. Material label-text changes fan out: affected labels re-issue, chained to their previous version.

✓ What Gemini may do

  • Widen recall. gemini-2.5-flash maps “my tangy no-knead boule” onto sourdough bread — constrained to an enum of the catalog itself, then re-checked by the same deterministic normalizer before any rule runs.
  • Classify materiality. gemini-2.5-pro reads each law diff and calls it material, cosmetic, or immaterial — the hard problem in the subscription. Unclassifiable deltas default to material, the conservative direction.

✕ What it may never do

  • Invent a product or move one between categories.
  • Turn a refusal into a sale, or admit a label the byte-verbatim QA gate rejected.
  • Overrule the table. If model and table disagree, the table wins — and the disagreement is recorded on the ledger.

05 · The three criteria

Read it the way the rubric reads it

merkle root fa7758f0f920…

Three equal criteria, tie-broken in this order. Here is where each one lives in this build — and where it stops.

TIE-BREAK 1

Business Viability

A $19 one-time product with its whole order path built: landing, free statute-cited verdict, Stripe Checkout, webhook fulfillment, print-ready label delivery, public QR provenance — one codebase, no second copy of the decision logic.

The retention mechanic is the Law-Watch re-issue: recurring value is logged autonomous work on the ledger, not a newsletter.

Honest status: nothing has been sold and the service is not deployed. The rail is wired in Stripe test mode; flipping the key is a business milestone, not an engineering one.

Check: server/ — fulfill.ts refuses prohibited orders and flags refunds · npm run serve runs the storefront with no keys

TIE-BREAK 2

AI-Native Operations

The order path runs agent-operated end to end — intake, verdict, label composition, QA gate, delivery, law-watch, re-issue — every decision appended to a hash-chained, Ed25519-signed ledger under five test-enforced invariants.

Gemini makes real decisions inside hard limits: gemini-2.5-flash resolves products enum-locked to the catalog; gemini-2.5-pro classifies law-diff materiality, defaulting to material when unsure. The model proposes, the table decides, disagreements are recorded.

The offline proof runs a deterministic mock mirroring the exact Gemini response schema; the real adapters are wired and key-gated.

Check: npm run self-test → 12/12 · npm run verify-ledger → 78/78 signatures, tamper at seq 39

TIE-BREAK 3

Category Impact

Category 2 is Entrepreneurship & Job Creation, and every buyer of this product is a founder: someone turning a home kitchen into legal income, blocked by a question that costs more to answer than the business earns in its first month.

The impact metric is built to be ledger-sourced — home-food businesses cleared and kept compliant, counted from signed rows, not asserted. Impact includes the refusals: the founder it stops from launching illegally.

A claim about the future, not a result: nobody has used this yet, so no impact is claimed — only made measurable by construction for when they do.

Check: 28 golden verdicts spanning eligible / license-required / prohibited · public per-label provenance pages
